## The UN target for remittance costs (SDG 10.c)
Sustainable Development Goal 10 is to "Reduce inequality within and among countries." Its target 10.c reads: "By 2030, reduce to less than 3 per cent the transaction costs of migrant remittances and eliminate remittance corridors with costs higher than 5 per cent." In short, the UN goal has two parts: bring the global average cost of sending remittances below 3 percent, and make sure that in every corridor people can send money for 5 percent or less.

## Indicator 10.c.1
Progress on the target is measured by indicator 10.c.1, "Remittance costs as a proportion of the amount remitted." The World Bank is the custodian agency responsible for global monitoring, and the data come from its Remittance Prices Worldwide (RPW) database. The official series are based on sending $200: the average cost of sending $200 to a receiving country, from a sending country, and within a corridor, plus the SmaRT average cost of sending $200 in a corridor, each expressed as a percentage of the amount sent.

## How the 3 percent part is measured
According to the indicator metadata, the 3 percent component refers to the global average total cost of sending $200 (or the equivalent in the sending currency), expressed as a percentage of the amount sent. It is calculated as the simple average of the total cost of all transparent services in the RPW database.

## How the 5 percent part is measured
The metadata explains that eliminating corridors costing more than 5 percent should be understood as making it possible for senders in every corridor to send money for an average cost of 5 percent or less. For this, it is enough that each corridor has at least three services meeting a defined set of requirements (such as service quality and reach) whose average cost is 5 percent or less. This is measured with the World Bank's SmaRT (Smart Remitter Target) average: the average cost of the three cheapest transparent services in a corridor that meet minimum requirements. SmaRT services must also deliver in five days or less and be accessible to the sender.

## What "price" and "total cost" mean
The metadata defines the price of a remittance as the total cost to the end user of sending it. That total includes the fees charged to the sender plus the exchange-rate margin, which is how far the exchange rate given to the customer differs from the current interbank exchange rate. The average cost in a corridor is the simple average of the total costs (fee plus exchange rate margin) quoted by each provider.

## Transparent services
A remittance service counts as "transparent" when its sending cost can be split into its two components, the transfer fee and the foreign exchange margin. If a provider does not disclose the exchange rate applied to the transaction, the service is treated as not transparent, and non-transparent services are left out of the global average.

## Other definitions used in the indicator
The metadata describes an international remittance transfer as a cross-border, person-to-person payment of relatively low value, typically recurring payments by migrant workers to their families at home. A remittance service provider is a business that provides remittance services for a price, directly or through agents, including banks and money transfer operators. A money transfer operator is a non-deposit-taking payment service provider where the sender pays per transfer, as opposed to the provider debiting an account the sender holds with it.

## How the data are collected
The World Bank collects the data by "mystery shopping" providers, with each corridor's sample covering at least 80 percent of that corridor's market. The metadata lists the release calendar as March and September. The G20 has also endorsed the SDG 10.c cost targets; the earlier G8/G20 "5x5" objective aimed to cut the global average cost by 5 percentage points in 5 years.
