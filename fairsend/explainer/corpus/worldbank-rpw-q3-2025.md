## What Remittance Prices Worldwide is
Remittance Prices Worldwide (RPW) is a World Bank database, launched in September 2008, that tracks what it costs people to send money across borders along major "corridors" (a sending country paired with a receiving country). It covers 48 sending countries and 102 receiving countries, 358 corridors in total, and tracks four types of remittance service providers: banks, money transfer operators (MTOs, including traditional and fintech firms), mobile operators, and post offices. RPW is the reference used to measure progress toward global remittance cost targets, including UN Sustainable Development Goal target 10.c. This summary is based on Issue 54 of the RPW report, published in September 2025 with Q3 2025 data, which was the latest report on the World Bank data catalog as of September 2026.

## How RPW measures the cost of a transfer
RPW records the total cost of each service as two parts: the transfer fee and the foreign exchange (FX) margin, meaning how far the exchange rate given to the customer falls short of the interbank (mid-market) rate. Costs are expressed as a percentage of the amount sent. The headline figures are for sending $200 (or the local-currency equivalent), which the report describes as a representative size for a typical remittance. RPW also collects prices for sending $500 and reports them in an annex. The report notes that, across regions, fees account for a large portion of total remittance costs, and that non-digital services cost more than digital services in every region.

## Global average cost of sending $200 (Q3 2025)
In Q3 2025 the Global Average total cost of sending $200 was 6.36 percent, down from 6.49 percent in Q1 2025. The report notes the Global Average has stayed below 7.00 percent since Q1 2019, and that it has fallen by 3.31 percentage points since Q1 2009, when it was 9.67 percent. The Global Weighted Average, which weights each corridor by the estimated size of its remittance flows, was 5.04 percent in Q3 2025. The UN SDG target is a global average of 3 percent by 2030, so the Q3 2025 average remained about double the target.

## Digital versus non-digital remittances
In Q3 2025 the global average cost for digital remittances was 4.59 percent (down from 4.85 percent in Q1 2025), compared with 7.30 percent for non-digital remittances. Digital services made up 35 percent of all services RPW collected in Q3 2025. In RPW's definition, a digital remittance is sent through a payment instrument in an online or self-assisted way and received into a transaction account such as a bank account, mobile money account or e-money account. The index of digital-only money transfer operators was 3.54 percent in Q3 2025. The International MTO Index, which tracks MTOs present in at least 85 percent of RPW corridors, fell to 5.52 percent from 5.91 percent in Q1 2025.

## The SmaRT indicator (Smart Remitter Target)
The World Bank introduced the Smart Remitter Target (SmaRT) indicator in Q2 2016. It aims to reflect what a "savvy consumer" with sufficiently complete information could pay in each corridor. SmaRT is the simple average of the three cheapest qualifying services for sending the equivalent of $200 in a corridor, expressed as a percentage of the amount sent. To qualify, a service must be transparent, deliver the money in five days or less, and be reasonably accessible (for example, branches nearby for in-person services, or access to the bank account, phone or internet the service requires). The Global SmaRT Average was 3.29 percent in Q3 2025, unchanged from the previous release.

## SmaRT and the 5 percent corridor goal
The SmaRT average is used to track the part of the UN goal that says it should be possible to send money for 5 percent or less in every corridor. As of Q3 2025, 78 percent of the corridors RPW covers had SmaRT averages below 5 percent. Twenty corridors had no SmaRT-qualifying services at all, which the report links to problems with access, reach or competition; many of these corridors have low internet or transaction-account penetration.

## Costs by region (Q3 2025)
Costs differ a lot by the region receiving the money. In Q3 2025, Sub-Saharan Africa remained the most expensive region to send money to, at an average total cost of 8.46 percent. The Middle East, North Africa, Afghanistan and Pakistan region became the cheapest receiving region, at 5.11 percent, overtaking South Asia, where the average rose from 4.80 percent to 5.30 percent. Europe and Central Asia saw the largest decrease, from 7.94 percent to 6.80 percent. Among G20 receiving countries the average was 5.68 percent, with Mexico the cheapest at 4.53 percent. Sending from G8 countries cost 5.40 percent on average.

## Costs by type of provider
Banks remained the most expensive type of provider, with an average total cost of 14.99 percent for sending $200 in Q3 2025, and the report notes that banks have stayed firmly above the Global Average over time. Money transfer operators averaged 4.72 percent. The report also observes that bank services are much slower than money transfer operator and mobile operator services.

## Costs by how money is paid in and paid out
How the transfer is funded and delivered also affects cost. In Q3 2025, paying with a credit or debit card was the cheapest funding method at 4.39 percent, compared with 7.01 percent when paying in cash and 8.69 percent when funding from a bank account. On the receiving side, delivery to a debit card cost 3.61 percent, delivery to a mobile wallet 3.92 percent, cash pick-up 5.56 percent, and delivery to a bank account (regardless of the originating bank) 7.86 percent.

## Cost of sending $500
Because part of the cost is a fixed fee, sending a larger amount is usually cheaper as a percentage. In Q3 2025 the Global Average cost of sending $500 was 4.08 percent, compared with 6.36 percent for $200. The International MTO Index for $500 was 3.83 percent and the Global SmaRT Average for $500 was 2.24 percent. The report notes that the cost of sending $500 has followed a similar trend over time to the cost of sending $200.

## How often RPW data are published
RPW data come from a mystery-shopping exercise of providers in each corridor and are released as periodic snapshots labelled by quarter. Recent reports compare Q1 and Q3 of each year (the Q3 2025 report compares against Q1 2025), and the UN metadata for SDG indicator 10.c.1 lists the release calendar as March and September. A snapshot shows prices on the collection dates, not the price a provider offers on any particular day.
