## About this section
Section 1005.30 of Regulation E (12 CFR part 1005, subpart B, often called the Remittance Transfer Rule) sets out the definitions used by the federal rules on remittance transfers. The text below is quoted from the regulation as published in the eCFR (Title 12, current as of the September 14, 2026 issue) and on the CFPB's regulations website. "Except as otherwise provided, for purposes of this subpart, the following definitions apply."

## What counts as a remittance transfer
(e) Remittance transfer—(1) General definition. A "remittance transfer" means the electronic transfer of funds requested by a sender to a designated recipient that is sent by a remittance transfer provider. The term applies regardless of whether the sender holds an account with the remittance transfer provider, and regardless of whether the transaction is also an electronic fund transfer, as defined in § 1005.3(b).

## Transfers that are not covered ($15 or less)
(2) Exclusions from coverage. The term "remittance transfer" does not include:

(i) Small value transactions. Transfer amounts, as described in § 1005.31(b)(1)(i), of $15 or less.

(ii) Securities and commodities transfers. Any transfer that is excluded from the definition of electronic fund transfer under § 1005.3(c)(4).

In plain terms, a transfer of $15 or less is not a "remittance transfer" under the rule, so the rule's disclosure, cancellation and error-resolution protections apply only to transfers of more than $15.

## Sender and designated recipient
(g) "Sender" means a consumer in a State who primarily for personal, family, or household purposes requests a remittance transfer provider to send a remittance transfer to a designated recipient.

(c) "Designated recipient" means any person specified by the sender as the authorized recipient of a remittance transfer to be received at a location in a foreign country.

(d) "Preauthorized remittance transfer" means a remittance transfer authorized in advance to recur at substantially regular intervals.

## Who is a remittance transfer provider
(f) Remittance transfer provider—(1) General definition. "Remittance transfer provider" or "provider" means any person that provides remittance transfers for a consumer in the normal course of its business, regardless of whether the consumer holds an account with such person.

(a) "Agent" means an agent, authorized delegate, or person affiliated with a remittance transfer provider, as defined under State or other applicable law, when such agent, authorized delegate, or affiliate acts for that remittance transfer provider.

## The normal course of business threshold (500 transfers a year)
(2) Normal course of business—(i) Safe harbor. For purposes of paragraph (f)(1) of this section, a person is deemed not to be providing remittance transfers for a consumer in the normal course of its business if the person:

(A) Provided 500 or fewer remittance transfers in the previous calendar year; and

(B) Provides 500 or fewer remittance transfers in the current calendar year.

A company that falls under this safe harbor is not a "remittance transfer provider," so the federal remittance rules do not apply to its transfers.

## The threshold used to be 100 transfers
The CFPB's official interpretation of this section explains the history of the threshold: "On July 21, 2020, the safe harbor threshold in § 1005.30(f)(2)(i) changed from 100 remittance transfers to 500 remittance transfers." Before that date, a person that provided 100 or fewer remittance transfers in the previous and current calendar years was deemed not to be providing remittance transfers in the normal course of its business. Since July 21, 2020, the number is 500.

## Transition period when a company crosses the threshold
(ii) Transition period—coming into compliance. Beginning on July 21, 2020, if a person that provided 500 or fewer remittance transfers in the previous calendar year provides more than 500 remittance transfers in the current calendar year, and if that person is then providing remittance transfers for a consumer in the normal course of its business pursuant to paragraph (f)(1) of this section, the person has a reasonable period of time, not to exceed six months, to begin complying with this subpart. Compliance with this subpart will not be required for any remittance transfers for which payment is made during that reasonable period of time.

## Business day
(b) "Business day" means any day on which the offices of a remittance transfer provider are open to the public for carrying on substantially all business functions.

## Covered and non-covered third-party fees
(h) Third-party fees. (1) "Covered third-party fees." The term "covered third-party fees" means any fees imposed on the remittance transfer by a person other than the remittance transfer provider except for fees described in paragraph (h)(2) of this section.

(2) "Non-covered third-party fees." The term "non-covered third-party fees" means any fees imposed by the designated recipient's institution for receiving a remittance transfer into an account except if the institution acts as an agent of the remittance transfer provider.

In other words, fees charged by the recipient's own bank for receiving the money into an account are "non-covered" third-party fees, while other fees charged by third parties along the way are "covered" third-party fees that the provider must disclose.
